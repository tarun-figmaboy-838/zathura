# Driftbound

A 3D space shooter that runs in the browser, built with Three.js, with models generated by headless Blender
and the title and button art designed in Figma.

The game opens on the bridge of your gunship, looking out through the windshield. Press Play and the
camera leaves the cockpit to watch the ship released from the Haven-9 station, then it dives into a black
hole. On the far side there are **12 levels**, taken from *Angle Hunt* (angle-game.vercel.app): before
every level the ship makes a short playable journey through an asteroid belt and flies through the
level's gate. The gate takes turns: the jagged, mountain-like crack of a broken planet (dark crust,
glowing veins, a molten core; its colours change each sector, `src/world/brokenPlanet.js`) or the
lit docking tunnel of a turning ring space station (`src/world/stationGate.js`). Both stay out of the
middle of the view while you fly, and the level's name shows on a banner. On the journey, asteroids are moving obstacles in the flight path, each timed to be on
your line just as you get there, tumbling as they come: a rock sweeping in from the side and across
your path, two rocks crossing from opposite sides one after the other, a boulder drifting onto your
line, or (from level 2) a rock wall with one gap that slides sideways so the gap moves; plus some
space junk. Later levels move faster. They are light stone with a soft warm rim so they read against the calm, sparse sky
without glaring, and they grow in rather than pop up; a rock about to hit the ship gets a red ring
with a "!" (the two soonest only; steer away and it goes). Fly round them or shoot them; the far belt
beside the lane is dim scenery only. Rock and junk crashes cost shield and hull but can never destroy the
ship (the hull stops at 15%), and the ship is repaired as it flies through the gate, so only the
battles decide a level. Along the way a few satellites and small outposts drift by off to the
sides, and now and then a friendly UFO flies alongside for a few seconds: the little green alien in
its dome waves and says hello ("Hey!", "Nice ship!"), then zips off with a goodbye. They cannot be
shot or locked onto (`src/world/props.js`). The levels come in three sectors of four; each sector ends with a meteor field and a
quick look at the stars earned. After the third sector comes the boss, the
Warden: a giant virus drone with one huge eye on top (its core) and four turrets
(`src/enemies/wardenDrone.js`), and then you jump home.

Music is synthesized live and changes with the action (cruise, battle, boss).

The first time something new appears (the gate, a virus drone, the battle radar, each angle rule, the
meteor field and its buttons, the boss) the game introduces it: one short line in the narration box,
with a drone camera shot that zooms in on it and back out. Tap the line to skip ahead. Every fresh visit introduces everything again.

After the ship crosses the black hole's horizon it rides a wormhole: a real 3D funnel anchored
where it opens (inside the hole), seen
opening as the ship approaches. Swirling gas, light streaks and sparks rush past, and the light at
the far end swells until it carries the ship out (`src/world/wormhole.js`).

The ending: after the Warden a giant portal opens; the ship races into it and vanishes like a time
jump, the portal implodes behind it, and the victory screen rates the run out of 3 stars.

## Angle battles

Each level is one virus drone, a robot shaped like a virus, so students only ever destroy machines,
never anything alive. Each sector brings a new kind, introduced by the co-pilot the first time, with
its own look, colour, names, lines and voice (`src/enemies/droneKinds.js`): the spiked **virus drone**
with one glowing eye (levels 1-4), the six-legged **Phage** with a gem head (levels 5-8), and the
**Swarm**, three little drones circling a hub that talk as "we" (mastery 1-4). It flies in,
holds position and keeps firing short bursts at you. The old raider ships with alien pilots and the Warden's
carrier are kept as a draft: set `ENEMY_LOOK` in `src/enemies/look.js` to `'ship'` to bring them back. Every
question has a time limit (25 s on L1, easing down to 15 s; 18 s for the Warden): if it runs out, the drone
destroys your ship and you retry the level. A radar ring is drawn around your ship in thin, crisp
lines (the stars and glow dim during battles so it reads clearly). No timer is shown; in the last
seconds the clock ticks and the screen edge pulses red, then the drone fires: a bright beam shows where your
cannon points, a red line runs to the enemy, and there is one marked angle and an **x** angle. Pick the
value of **x** and the cannon turns by that angle: the right answer lands on the enemy and destroys it,
and the question card shows the working (for example "76° + 104° = 180°"). A wrong answer misses, the
enemy fires back, and the card shows the rule (after a second miss, the working).

Layout: the question bar on top, the radar in the middle and the answers along the bottom (one row on
landscape screens, 2×2 on phones). Comms are a speech bubble on the craft that is talking (the drone,
the Warden, or your own ship for the co-pilot), placed so it never covers the question, the answers or
the score; phones get a compact bubble.

Enemies talk back, like comms chatter in an arcade shooter, in a game-style dialogue box with character
portraits (made with Figma AI) that act out each line: each raider gets a silly callsign, taunts
you when it arrives, mocks a wrong answer and panics when hit, and the Warden boasts and sulks
(`src/game/banter.js`). The timer waits while someone is talking. After a defeat the enemy gloats and
the co-pilot reminds you of the rule.

| Levels | Rules (Angle Hunt) |
|---|---|
| L1–L8 | Hand-made: linear pairs (angles on a straight line add up to 180°) and vertically opposite angles (equal) |
| M1–M4 | Mastery: the same two rules with new random angles every run |
| Warden | Its 4 turrets, then its core (3 hits); every target is a mastery question |

Every 4 levels the stars you earned pop up for a moment (every target hit on the first try = 3,
at least 60% = 2, otherwise 1); the victory screen rates the whole run out of 3 stars.

## Voice-over

Every comms line, the co-pilot's introductions and tips are voiced with **Kokoro** (open source,
Apache-2.0, runs locally for free). Each character has its own voice: a goofy pitched-up raider, a
deep metallic Warden, a cheerful robot co-pilot on the radio, with comic delivery per kind of line
(panic is fast and squeaky, gloating is slow). The music dips while someone speaks.

Files live in `assets/vo/` (named by a hash of the line's text, listed in `manifest.json`). After
changing a line, regenerate them: `python tools/vo/make_vo.py` (Python 3.10-3.12 with
`pip install kokoro soundfile scipy numpy`; uses macOS `afconvert`). A line without a voice file
just plays the talk blips.

## Play

The game has to be served over http. Opening `index.html` directly won't load the 3D models.

- **Mac:** double-click `start.command`. The first time, macOS may block it: right-click it, choose
  **Open**, then **Open** again.
- **Windows:** double-click `start.bat` (needs Python from python.org).
- **Any system with Python 3:** run `python3 serve.py` in this folder, then open http://127.0.0.1:8123

`serve.py` tells the browser not to cache, so a normal reload always runs the latest code. With a
plain `python3 -m http.server`, reload with Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+R after changing files.

Use a recent Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari. You need an internet connection, because the Three.js engine
and fonts load from public CDNs.

## Deploy (Vercel)

It's a static site with no build step. `vercel.json` sets caching for the models, and `.vercelignore`
leaves out the Blender scripts and local launchers.

- **CLI:** run `vercel` in this folder (choose framework **Other**, no build command, output directory `.`).
- **Dashboard:** import the repository and set **Root Directory** to this folder, framework **Other**.

## Controls

| Action | Mouse / touch | Keyboard | On-screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| Answer a battle question | tap an answer | 1 – 4 | Answer buttons |
| Fly the ship (journeys and meteor fields) | – | arrow keys or W A S D | Joystick |
| Fire at the target X | – | Space | Fire button |
| Skip the intro | click | Space / Enter / Esc | Skip button |
| Pause / mute | – | Esc / M | Pause button (the pause menu also has sound on/off and back to the station) |

On the journeys and in the meteor fields only the joystick and FIRE show. The target sight (a
turning segmented ring, four ticks and a bold **X**) sits ahead of the ship and moves with it,
leading the way you steer. When a rock, junk or a drone is near it, it locks on with two quick beeps:
the sight snaps tight, spins faster and turns gold, and gold brackets with a **LOCK** tag frame the
target, with a health bar for rocks that take several hits. Each hit flashes the brackets white and
knocks a bar off; when the target breaks the brackets fly apart. If the locked target is about to hit
you, everything turns red and the tag reads **DANGER**: dodge it or shoot it. There is no mouse or tap aiming. The ring around the Fire
button shows the cannon's heat. In the angle battles the controls hide and the answer buttons take over.

## Project layout

```
index.html        page, HUD and on-screen controls
src/              game code (Three.js ES modules)
assets/models/    ship, raider, boss, station and asteroid models (.glb, normals and colours stored compactly)
assets/ui/        title, cockpit, button art and character portraits, all WebP (made with Figma AI; the answer capsules are drawn in CSS after the POLYGON kit)
assets/vo/        the voice lines (.m4a: AAC plays in every browser, Safari included) and manifest.json
blender/          the Python scripts that generate every model, and compact_glb.py (makes them smaller)
PLAN.md           design plan and milestones
```

## Rebuilding the models (optional)

Install Blender (the scripts were built and tested with Blender 5.2), then from this folder run:

```
BLENDER=/path/to/blender ./blender/build_all.sh
```

After exporting, it runs `blender/compact_glb.py`, which stores each model's normals as bytes and its
vertex colours as one byte per channel (the glTF `KHR_mesh_quantization` extension, read natively by
three.js). The shapes, pivots and names stay exactly the same; the files are about 30% smaller.
